The problem

Signals live in one tool. Accounts in another. Relationships in people's heads. Actions in a sequencer. Outcomes nowhere.

Teams already have CRM, enrichment, sequencers, and alerts. The gap is the handoff: who notices the signal, checks context, chooses the action, and gets it approved before committing.

How Dominion works

Capture → Prioritise → Route → Govern

01

Capture

Connect the buying signals your team already collects from selected sources.

02

Prioritise

Apply prioritisation rules for fit, urgency, and available context.

03

Route

Recommend the next action and the person responsible for it.

04

Govern

Keep the workflow aligned with human approval rules and operator oversight before anything is sent.

What you get

The 30-day guided pilot

The proposed path starts with one customer-provided CSV signal export and an operator approval gate. Additional connectors require technical validation and written scope.

Ownership

Deploy in your environment

Target deployment is customer-controlled. Data, credentials, hosting, handover, and rights to custom work are confirmed in the written scope.

Governed

Human-in-the-loop gate

Recommended actions stay visible and reviewable. Nothing is executed without explicit human approval.

Open-source core · optional services

Self-hostable software. Separately scoped delivery.

The core is MIT-licensed software you can inspect and self-host. Aethonex deployment and customization are separately scoped services, not a subscription required to use the core.

MIT-licensed core

  • Source is public and inspectable
  • Self-host in an environment you operate
  • Pilot-grade workflow foundation, not a hosted SaaS
  • Human approval is part of the documented workflow
  • Hosting and provider costs are yours to scope

Aethonex services

  • Proposed $4,500 fee for the defined pilot scope
  • Self-host the MIT-licensed core in your environment
  • You retain your data, credentials, and infrastructure
  • Approval gates are part of the documented workflow
  • Hosting and external-provider costs are separate
