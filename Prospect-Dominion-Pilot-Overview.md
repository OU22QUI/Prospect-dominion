# Prospect Dominion — Pilot Overview

## Purpose

The purpose of the Pilot is to prove that the system is valuable in a real operating context without requiring a full production rollout at first.

## Pilot scope

A pilot should be intentionally bounded to a limited set of:
- accounts or segments
- stakeholder users
- workflows
- data sources
- deployment environment

## Suggested pilot structure

### Duration
- 4 to 8 weeks

### Goal
- validate signal-to-action utility
- prove operational trust and governance
- confirm account prioritization quality
- establish the decision path for deployment or expansion

### Success criteria
- signal quality is understandable to operators
- account prioritization is credible and actionable
- relationship and warm-intro context improves confidence
- human approvals are manageable
- workflow actions are aligned with product and sales intent

## Included items
- deployment configuration in a pilot environment
- product onboarding and workflow mapping
- basic environment-level branding if needed
- support and review checkpoints
- documented success criteria and conversion plan

## Excluded items
- full custom feature development without approval
- broad multi-environment rollout
- long-term white-label engineering outside agreed scope
- support assumptions not clearly defined in the pilot term

## Conversion to deployment

The pilot should end with a straightforward handoff:
- what worked,
- what was learned,
- what should expand,
- what can be formalized in deployment,
- what should be omitted or deferred.

This keeps the pilot from becoming an open-ended experiment and makes expansion feel natural.

## Recommended commercial framing

Treat the pilot as the lowest-friction commercial conversion path. It should be easy to say yes to, clear in scope, and easy to evaluate against business outcomes.
